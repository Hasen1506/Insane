# v0.6: chat, a feed, going dark, and the city at night

v0.6 adds what you asked for: chat, a public feed, Vanish as an app lock, scheduled messages, and more kinds of city play. Each was simulated before it was built, and each comes in two forms:

- the **loud** form people already know (one group room, open DMs, likes and comments, first come first served);
- a **quiet** form.

The quiet forms win every time. The loud forms are where the harm is.

The base is unchanged from v0.5. This round is additions, not a redo.

## How it was tested

| Model | What it answers | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| App engine (v0.5 engine plus new switches) | Chat, feed, sealed messages, Go dark, first pact in your own words | 32 paired seeds, two worlds |
| Decode model | Do circles learn to wait for others to crack a clue? | 60 circles, 12 weekly rounds, 40 seeds |
| Drops model | Where drops go, and whether arriving first wins | 100 circles in 6 zones, 40 seeds |
| Play-type model | 16 kinds of city play, 12 weeks each | 100 circles, 40 seeds |
| Fake-account cost sum | What each check makes a faker buy or do | Not a simulation |

**The two worlds.** In the calibrated model, v0.5 already sits near 90% on day 30, so new ideas have little room to show. Every idea is also run in a "busier people" world, where every reason to open the app counts half. In that world v0.5 starts at about 80%.

**Noise.** A switch that changes nothing moved day 30 by −0.3 and −0.0 points. Treat differences under about 1 point as noise.

With every new switch off, the engine reproduces v0.5 exactly.

## Your questions

### Vanish: just the app, or the whole phone?

**No app can blank or lock the whole phone.** Not on iPhone, and not on Android without being the phone's device manager.

**What an app can do** is cover chosen apps with its own screen:

- **iPhone.** Since iOS 16, Apple's Screen Time API lets an app ask the phone's owner for permission to shield apps. The owner confirms with Face ID or the passcode. It can then cover the apps you pick, or all apps and websites, with a custom screen.
  - Apps in your Screen Time "Always Allowed" list stay open, and Apple says the device can't be locked this way. Developers report that Phone, Messages and Settings stay open too.
  - Opal and one sec work this way.
- **Android.** App blockers use "usage access" to see which app is open, then draw a block screen over it. Some also use the accessibility service, which Google Play allows with an in-app disclosure, consent and a declaration.
  - Full kiosk lock needs the phone to be managed by an organisation. A normal app only gets screen pinning, which the user can exit.

**So v0.6's Go dark:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Duration | 1 to 24 hours, never more |
| What goes dark | "noisy apps" (social, video, games) or "everything except calls, messages, maps and camera" |
| Together | optionally with the circle ("Dark Sunday") |
| While dark | INSANE shows only a countdown and who's dark with you; calls and emergency numbers always work |
| Coming back early | the circle sees "1 h of 6", with no strike and no stake |
| Coming back | is the moment the app is for: what did everyone do? |

**What the model says:** about zero for retention, the same as v0.5's Vanish. It's built because it's good for people, and because "we went dark together" is a story the circle tells, not because it keeps anyone.

### Onboarding: predetermined wording → needs, wants and urges

The last onboarding step is now **What's on your mind?** It has three tabs (**Need**, **Want**, **Urge**), a text box and example chips.

INSANE reads the line with simple rules on the phone; nobody reads it unless you send it. It shows "How INSANE reads it" (Becomes / What / When / Proof) before anything starts. The rules:

| You type | It becomes |
|---|---|
| A need, with or without a frequency ("run 3 times a week") | **Pact**: 21 days, two free misses a week |
| A want with money words ("go to Lisbon in May", "buy a PS5") | **Plan + pledge pot** |
| A want without money | **Plan** with its own thread |
| An urge involving fear ("swim in the sea in winter", "sing karaoke in public") | **Ladder**: private rungs; physical ones end at a Zone |
| An urge to confess or tell someone something | **Midnight**: unsigned, or sealed to open later |
| An urge to get away with something | **Mole task** from the harmless list |
| Anything else | **Dare** in the circle's jar, starting at 3 out of 10 |
| Anything violent | Refused, with a suggestion to try it as a ladder or a dare |
| Anything about self-harm | A support message (Samaritans 116 123 in the UK and Ireland, or a local crisis line), and nothing is created |

**What the model says:** about zero, because most people start a pact in week one anyway. The real test is whether people finish the thing they typed. A model can't see that; five people trying it can. An AI reader could later handle messier sentences, but it would mean sending people's urges to a server, so rules on the phone come first.

### Map clues that reveal as more circles decode

This became **Decode**, a weekly night in the City.

**How a night runs:**

1. A clue goes out at 19:00, and circles type their answer into the app.
2. Public hints unlock as more circles solve it (3, 8, then 15 solves).
3. A full-reveal timer counts down and **gets faster with every solve**, so circles that can't crack it still get to go.
4. The full reveal shows only an **area**. The exact spot is a small clue on site, or the reward for solving it yourself.

**The anti-lazy rules:**

- **Points go by solve order** (100, 93, 86…), never by who arrives first.
- A prize is drawn among everyone who turns up in the hour, weighted by points.
- You see public hints only after your circle has had a go.
- A circle that has solved it can **pass a hint** to a circle that's stuck, and both score 15.

**What the model says about laziness.** Circles copy whichever strategy paid off better each week:

| Clue | Circles waiting by week 12 | Effect of hint-gating and passing hints on |
|---|---|---|
| Easy: an average circle cracks it in about an hour | 3% | barely changes it |
| Twice as hard, and the effort feels like work | about 25% | barely changes it |

So the lever against laziness is **clue difficulty**: tune clues so most circles crack them in about an hour, and make the second hint generous. Passing a hint on doesn't stop waiting, but it raises the share of weaker circles that score from 22% to 26%.

**The real danger is racing.** When arriving first wins:

| | Rushing | Busiest 10 minutes at the spot |
|---|---|---|
| Arriving first wins | 100% of arrivals | about 100 people |
| Points by solve order | 6% | about 60 people |

### Sponsors, auctions, a shrinking zone or a walk-through?

| Design | Circles in reach | Outskirts in reach | Busiest 10 min | Rushing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One big drop, first come | 73% | 35% | 235 people | 70% |
| One per zone, first come | 100% | 100% | 160 | 71% |
| Shrinking zone | 83% | 55% | 159 | 70% |
| Shops bid for the spots | 82% | 41% | 33 | 4% |
| Trail: 5 stops, any order, 2 hours | 92% | 72% | 8 | 4% |
| **One per zone, sized to it, any time in the hour, prize drawn** | **100%** | **100%** | **46** | **4%** |

Built: **drops in every zone, sized to how many circles live there, with an hour's window and a draw.** The **Trail** is the walk-through version.

**Sponsors:**

- Shops pay to host a drop in their own venue. Pokémon GO's sponsored locations work this way: McDonald's Japan in 2016, Starbucks in December 2016. In 2017 Niantic said sponsors paid "less than $0.50" per daily unique visit.
- **INSANE places the drops where circles live.** Auctioning the spots puts them all in the middle of town.
- Players never pay to enter. v0.4 showed that's a no-go.

**The shrinking zone is out.** It combines rushing with crowding.

**Why racing and chasing are out, with real examples:**

- **Pokémon GO.** In its first 148 days it was linked to 286 extra crashes in one Indiana county, 134 of them near PokéStops (Faccio & McConnell, Purdue, 2017).
- **Central Park, July 2016.** A rare Pokémon sent a crowd sprinting into the park at night.
- **Missouri, July 2016.** Four teens used lures to draw players to secluded spots and robbed them at gunpoint.
- **Forrest Fenn's treasure.** At least four people died searching for it before it was found in 2020.

### Chat: within circles, and with other circles

The model is clear:

| Chat design | Day 30 |
|---|---|
| One busy room for the whole circle | about −6 points |
| Threads | 0 to +3 |

Threads still beat one room at a quarter of the assumed notification noise.

**What v0.6 builds:**

- **Circle → Chat.** Threads live on a plan, pot or pact, and only ping the people in that thing. **General** is muted unless someone @s you. You can keep your group chat.
- **Rooms with other circles.** Answering a Call opens a room for the two circles. It closes 48 hours after the thing is done.
- **People you've met.** You can message someone from another circle only after you've scanned phones in person, for example at a Zone night or a drop. Either of you can close the chat, and it closes for both.
- **No cold DMs.** Open DMs between strangers brought back about the same number of people as rooms, plus about 180 people a season getting messages they didn't want. Ofcom's 2023 survey found three in five UK 11 to 18 year olds had had potentially uncomfortable contact online.

### People like to share with everyone: a public feed?

**Yes, without the scoreboard.** City → **Feed**:

- Anyone in a verified circle can post what they did or made.
- Reactions are "❤", "we're in" and "steal this", with **no numbers shown to anyone, the poster included**.
- There are no follower counts.
- No comments from strangers; if you want in, you answer with a Call.

It was the best single idea this round: +2.2, and +5.5 in the busier world. The same feed with likes, followers and comments added less (+0.7 / +4.0) and hurt about 220 people. Meta's own 2020 research, reported by the WSJ in 2021, found that 32% of teen girls who already felt bad about their bodies said Instagram made them feel worse.

### Scheduled release for confessions and gossip

Midnight now has two modes:

- **Unsigned.** It still goes out with the batch, at 00:00, once at least three are waiting. You can now pick **"some night this week"** as well as tonight or tomorrow. A wider window means fewer authors worked out: 48 against 78.
- **Sealed, opens later.** It's **signed with your name**, so you *can* pick the exact moment: when your pact ends, New Year's Eve, in 3 months, or your circle's first birthday. Nobody can read it before then, you included.

An unsigned message never gets an exact time. v0.5 showed that picking the minute gets about five times as many authors worked out.

### Can the QR scan be faked? Fingerprints? Proof of life?

**A static QR code can be faked**: screenshot it and forward it. What each extra check does (each row adds to the one above; budgets: troll £0 and 2 hours, scammer £300 and 20 hours, farm £5,000 and 300 hours):

| Check | Faking it takes | Troll | Scammer | Farm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sign-up only | 4 accounts on one phone | 6 | 66 | 1,000 |
| Static QR | screenshot and forward | 5 | 50 | 750 |
| Rotating code (every 15 s, like Ticketmaster's SafeTix) | a live relay; emulators on one laptop still work | 2 | 25 | 375 |
| + Bluetooth handshake, same room | 4 real phones (reusable) | 0 | 25 | 375 |
| + genuine-app check, one account per phone (Apple App Attest, Google Play Integrity) | fresh phones per circle | 0 | 1 | 31 |
| + real phone number, no VoIP | SIMs | 0 | 1 | 28 |
| + 14 days' probation: one real meet-up with an established circle before posting | showing up in person | 0 | 0 | 21 |
| + liveness selfie, Mission hosts only | a real face per hosting circle | 0 | 0 | 5 |

**Default:** rotating code + Bluetooth + genuine-app check + phone number. To the user that's one tap together and one SMS code. Then probation before posting.

**Fingerprints and Face ID can't help.** Your phone tells an app only "the owner unlocked it", never who you are, so they can't stop one person running two phones.

**Selfie liveness** is the strongest check, but it's biometric data:

- Illinois' BIPA allows damages of $1,000–$5,000 per violation; Facebook paid $650M in 2021.
- GDPR Article 9 treats biometric identification as special-category data.

So selfie liveness is for Mission hosts only, if at all.

**Orb-style proof of personhood is too much.** World's orb scanning was suspended in Kenya (2023), Spain and Portugal (2024), and Indonesia (2025).

## What's built in the phone

- **Onboarding step 4:** Need / Want / Urge, your own words, "How INSANE reads it".
- **Circle → Chat** (threads, General muted), **Midnight** (unsigned this week / sealed), Plans, Pacts, Mole.
- **City → Map:**
  - the Decode area ring shrinks as more circles solve; type the answer and pass a hint on;
  - the Trail;
  - three drops, one per zone, with sponsor and draw rules.
- **City → Feed** without counts. **Calls** open rooms; **People you've met** have a Message button.
- **+ → Go dark** (1 to 24 h, what goes dark, with the circle) shows a dark countdown screen, then "come back early" or "skip to the end".
- **+ → Seal a message** and **+ → Post to the City feed.**

## Caveats

- **Guesses:** notification noise, harassment rates, play-type fun and the 1-in-4 chance of fakers being spotted. The chat result holds across a 4× range of noise. Change the play-type table above and the ranking moves.
- **Levels:** the calibrated model saturates near 90%. Read the busier world and the comparisons, not the levels.
- **The real test:**
  - five people through onboarding, to see whether the needs/wants/urges screen reads them right;
  - one real Decode night with 10 circles, timing how long the clue takes;
  - one Dark Sunday, then asking what people did.
