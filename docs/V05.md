# v0.5: a reason to stay, and a flow that makes sense

Your questions, compressed: *How is the app structured? Missions can be faked, so do we need escrow? Will other circles' wins motivate people, given that everything goes to Instagram? What about targets, anonymous gossip, working with other circles, and AI-made fake circles? How do Vanish and fear work, is the Hunt safe, and how should "get away with something" work? Why are favors valued at random? Why isn't this just WhatsApp? What's the reason to keep opening it? And the app feels clunky, so what's next?*

**Short answer**
- **The reason to persist is Pacts, not stunts.** You won't do something insane every day. You will check in on a goal your circle is watching. Pacts were the biggest gain tested: **+8.6 points** of day-30 retention on top of v0.4.
- **The best "get away with something" is the Mole:** one secret saboteur in the circle each week, with harmless tasks and a vote on Sunday. **+6.0.** It gives the Hunt's thrill without anyone chasing anyone.
- **The rules decide whether an idea helps or hurts.** Strict pacts (no misses, public strikes) *lose* 4.4 points. Anonymous gossip about other people hurts about five times as many people as messages about yourself, for almost no gain.
- **Fake Missions are stopped by reputation, not money.** Only circles that have met in person can propose, they stake Reliability, three other circles confirm, and a Zone approves. That cuts wasted trips to fakes from 128 to 5 and people lured to about zero. Money escrow does worse and puts the app back in the money-holding business.
- **The clunkiness was real, and fixing it matters as much as any feature.** Shown all at once, the new features fall *below* v0.4 once each new thing to learn costs people a little attention. Merged into fewer nouns and unlocked over two weeks, nearly all the gain survives.

The phone in [`prototype/index.html`](../prototype/index.html) is rebuilt around this. It opens on your first five minutes. v0.4 is kept at [`prototype/v4/`](../prototype/v4/index.html). The first console tab, **v0.5, tested**, has every chart and table below.

---

## 1. How the app is structured now

```
You ─ vouched in by someone ─▶ your Circle (4–12 people you actually see)
                                 │  Plans · Pacts · Midnight · the Mole · Pots · Favors · Dares
                                 ▼
                               the City (other circles nearby)
                                    Map (Zones, Missions, Hunts, Drops) · Wall · Calls · League
```

**The circle is the unit.** Everything personal happens inside it. The City is where circles meet each other, never strangers one-to-one.

**Favors: inside the circle, or across circles via a Call.** There is no open marketplace of strangers. If Third Floor needs a projector, they post a Call, someone from your circle answers, and the favor settles in hours the same way.

### The first five minutes

| Step | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Invite | "Maya brought you in." Her Reliability rides on you for 21 days | Invite-only and accountable: the first defence against fakes |
| 2. Your mask | Pick a handle for Midnight and Zone nights | "Be someone else," made a feature |
| 3. Your circle | Scan each other's phones next time you're together | Proves you're real people who've met. Unlocks posting in the City |
| 4. First pact | "What do you keep saying you'll do?" Pick one and a stake | Gives day 2 a reason to exist |

### Five buttons, not five worlds

| v0.4 | v0.5 |
|---|---|
| City · Today · Chapter · League · Money | **Home · Circle · + · City · Ledger** |
| Chapter was a second group with its own rules and Vault | **Merged into Circle.** Vouching and 24-hour clips are circle features now |
| League was a tab | A segment of City |
| Things to do were spread over five tabs | **Home is one list** of whatever needs you today |
| Creating something meant finding the right tab | **One + button**: dare, favor, plan, pot, Midnight, Call, Mission |
| Homework | **Ladder pacts** (a pact with steps instead of a habit) |
| Groceries were favors | **Groceries are Pots.** Favors are only time |
| Everything visible on day one | **Unlocked as the circle grows**: circle basics first, then pacts and Midnight, then the City |

**Concepts a new person must learn:** v0.4 had 13 on day one. v0.5 has 14 in total, but only 3 on day one, 7 by day five and all 14 after two weeks.

## 2. Why here and not WhatsApp

WhatsApp is where you **talk about** doing things. INSANE is where they **get done**. It holds things a chat can't:
- a pot that only goes ahead if six people commit;
- a ledger of who did what, in hours;
- a pact with check-ins and a stake;
- a fixture with a lineup;
- messages released together at midnight so nobody knows who wrote them;
- proof that a circle has met in person.

So v0.5 has **no chat on purpose**. Every plan says "talk it through in your group chat." Building chat would make INSANE a worse WhatsApp. Leaving it out makes it the thing WhatsApp groups are missing.

**And Instagram?** Instagram is where things go to be **seen**. INSANE is where they **start**. Feats can be shared out as story cards, with faces blurred unless everyone in the clip says yes. INSANE doesn't try to be a feed.

## 3. The reason to keep opening it

The stupid stuff is rare by nature, so the app needs a rhythm:

| How often | What | Why it brings you back |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Pact check-in · Daily Dare | One tap; your circle sees the tick |
| A few times a week | Midnight | Something new and honest from people you know |
| Weekly | The Mole · fixture and Zone Night · Market Day | A secret, a team, a table |
| Monthly | Mission · a Pot for something big | The story you tell |
| Each season | Insane Week (where **Vanish** lives) | The legend |

**The stupid stuff is why people join. Pacts and the ledger are why they stay. The money and the people are why they can't leave.**

## 4. Your questions, one by one

### Missions dropped by anyone: they can be fake. Escrow?
The danger of a fake Mission isn't lost money, since nobody ever pays to join. It's **being sent somewhere you shouldn't be**. So the defence is about who can post and where:
- Only circles that have **met in person** can propose a Mission. Anyone can join one.
- The host circle **stakes 3 Reliability**. If it doesn't happen as described, they lose it and can't host again.
- It goes live only when **3 other circles confirm**. A **Zone partner** approves it, and files a permit for 50+ people.
- **Public places from the Zone's list, in daylight.** The exact spot goes only to people who said yes, two hours before.

| Rule | Day 30 | Wasted trips to fakes | People lured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anyone can post | +0.1 | 128 | 1.4 |
| Met in person to *join* anything | −1.8 | 48 | 0.5 |
| Met in person to post | +0.7 | 48 | 0.3 |
| Host stakes Reliability | +0.5 | 31 | 0.03 |
| **Met in person to post + stake** *(built)* | **+0.7** | **5** | **0.03** |
| Host pays a money deposit | 0.0 | 53 | 0.4 |
| Deposit + met in person + stake | −0.1 | 1 | 0 |

Over 120 days in a city of 1,800 people.

**Escrow loses.** A deposit stops fewer fakes than a reputation stake. It halves the number of real Missions, because honest hosts don't want to put money down. And holding deposits makes INSANE a money business again (see [`OPTIONS.md`](OPTIONS.md)). Reputation costs a faker more than money does, because it can't be bought back.

**Circle-made Missions barely move retention** (+0.7). They're for growth and stories. The official monthly Mission stays; circle proposals are extra.

### Will other circles' wins motivate people?
Yes, modestly: **+1.5 to +1.9**. More importantly, it compounds. The Wall turns other circles' Pacts, Moles and Missions into ideas you can **steal**. Removing the Wall from the full v0.5 costs 2.1 points, more than it adds on its own.

Two design rules:
- **Circles your size, nearby, not a global leaderboard.** People compare themselves to people like them (social comparison theory). The model couldn't tell the two apart (+1.9 vs +1.5, within noise), so the research decides.
- **One card per thing a circle actually did.** No free posting, so it never becomes a feed to scroll.

**Does posting need to exist?** Two kinds of post, both structured: **Feats** ("look what we did") and **Calls** ("who's in?"). Free-form posting would turn INSANE into a worse Instagram and a moderation job.

### What can circles do together?
Calls are the primitive for working together:
- **a spare player** for a fixture (guests fill lineups, so small circles stop forfeiting);
- **co-builders** for a Hunt;
- **a lend** (a projector, a van), settled in hours;
- **co-hosting** a Mission (each co-host circle stakes);
- **League fixtures**, Market Day and Insane Week, where circles meet anyway.

Calls add +1.6, mostly for small circles that couldn't field a lineup. Money doesn't cross circles: Pots stay inside one circle.

### Targets within circles: Pacts
A pact is something you keep saying you'll do, with your circle as the people who check. It can be a **habit** ("run three times a week") or a **Ladder** (a fear, in steps). Stakes are a dare from the circle's jar or cooking for everyone, never money.

| | Day 30 | Pacts finished / failed | People who left because of pact pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pacts, two free misses a week, 4 strikes and it quietly ends** | **+8.6** | 1,853 / 267 | 2 |
| Strict pacts: no misses, public strikes | **−4.4** | 230 / 2,077 | 377 |

The same idea goes from best to worst on one rule. Outside the model, the evidence for accountability is real but softer than its reputation:
- Giné, Karlan and Zinman's commitment-account trial (2010) raised smokers' 6-month quit rate by about 3 percentage points.
- Gail Matthews' often-quoted study found 76% vs 43% goal achievement with weekly progress reports to a friend. It was a conference presentation and never peer-reviewed.

### Anonymous gossip: what to call it, and when to send it
Call it **Midnight**, because the name explains the mechanic. Messages go out together at 00:00, shuffled, once at least three are waiting.

**Gossip about other people is the version that kills apps.** Sarahah was pulled from both app stores in 2018 after bullying complaints. NGL settled with the US FTC in 2024 for $5 million and was banned from under-18s, partly for sending fake anonymous messages to sell subscriptions. So the rules are:
- **Only about yourself.** Names and descriptions of members are blocked before sending.
- **Anyone in the circle can remove a message for everyone**, without giving a reason.
- **You pick the night; the app picks the moment.** If you pick the exact time, people can match it to when you were on your phone.

| Version | Day 30 | People hurt | Authors worked out | People who left because of a message |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **About you, batched at 00:00** *(built)* | **+2.3** | 172 | 59 | 10 |
| About you, pick one of three slots | +3.2 | 175 | 180 | – |
| About you, pick the exact minute | +4.7 | 182 | 303 | – |
| About others, batched | +0.4 | 795 | 56 | – |
| About others, sent instantly | +1.1 | 817 | 668 | 162 |

Letting people pick the exact minute brings people back more often, but five times as many authors get found out. Three fixed slots is the compromise if people ask for it.

### "Be someone else"
That's the **handle**. You use it at Midnight and at Zone nights, and pick it in the first five minutes. Your circle knows your name; the City knows your handle.

### How Vanish and Feel Fear work
**Feel Fear is the Ladder pact.** Privately, you list steps up a fear: speaking up in a meeting, singing at karaoke, cold water, asking a stranger for directions in a language you're learning. Your circle sees "rung 4 of 7", never the rungs themselves. A spotter from the circle comes along for each rung.
- **Physical rungs** (heights, cold water) only count at a Zone, with staff.
- **Why it works:** fear is most enjoyable in the middle. A haunted-house study found enjoyment peaks at moderate fear (Andersen et al., *Psychological Science*, 2020). A ladder keeps each step in that sweet spot.
- **Result:** +2.5, better than Homework (+1.4).

**Vanish is a season event, not a feature.** Once a season, your circle sends one member off for a day: a sealed destination, the phone locked except for calls and maps, and a "keeper" in the circle who knows where they are. They come back with a story at Midnight.
- **Result: retention +0.3**, which is noise. A day off the app is a day off the app.
- **Where it lives:** Insane Week, as a legend people tell, not as something in the daily app.

### The Hunt
**Dropped.** You're right that it can cause harm. On 12 February 2025, in Nassau County, Florida, an off-duty state agent shot an 18-year-old playing Senior Assassin in the arm, mistaking a blue water gun for a real one. Chasing people through a city with anything that looks like a weapon is not fixable with rules.

### "Get away with something": the Mole
The bank-robbery fantasy, made harmless.
- Each week one member of a circle of 4 or more is secretly the Mole. They get three tasks from the app's list, such as getting three people to say "pineapple", swapping the fridge magnets around unseen, or wearing someone's borrowed hoodie to the fixture.
- On Sunday everyone votes. If fewer than half guess right, the Mole got away with it, and it can go on the Wall.
- **Rules:** tasks only come from the list. Nothing involving phones, money, bedrooms or anyone outside the circle. Anyone can sit a week out.
- **Result: +6.0**, second only to Pacts. Social deduction (Mafia, Werewolf, Among Us) has decades of evidence that people love it, and here it runs in real life, for a week.

### "Favors are valued randomly"
They were. v0.5 fixes it the way time banks have since Edgar Cahn's "Time Dollars" in the 1980s:
- **1 favor = 1 hour of someone's time**, in 15-minute steps. **Everyone's hour is worth the same.**
- The person asking suggests a length. Whoever helps can adjust it before it's logged. The app suggests the circle's usual length for that kind of favor ("moving furniture: usually 1 h").
- **Money never goes in favors.** Groceries, trips and purchases are Pots. Favors are only time and effort. That removes most disputes about worth.
- **The trade-off:** a plumber's hour counts the same as a dog walk. For skilled work, the helper can log more hours if the asker agrees. That's still time, never price.
- **Result: +1.4**, and it makes uneven swaps rarer.

### AI fake circles: should we connect Instagram?
**No.** It wouldn't work, and it would hurt.
- **It wouldn't work.** Meta shut down the Instagram Basic Display API on 4 December 2024. Third-party apps can no longer read ordinary personal accounts, only business and creator ones. Fake Instagram accounts are cheap anyway.
- **It would hurt.** It links people's dares and Midnight to their public identity, which is exactly the harm to avoid.

What actually stops fake circles:
1. **Invite-only, with a vouch.** Every account traces back to a real member who staked Reliability on it.
2. **Meeting in person.** A circle scans each other's phones in the same place to unlock posting. A bot farm can fake profiles; it can't cheaply fake four people in one room. Zone staff can verify at Zone nights too.
3. **Clips recorded in the app only.** No uploads, so AI-generated video has no way in.
4. **Gate posting, not joining.** Requiring the in-person scan before you can *join* City events costs about 2 points of retention. Requiring it only to *post* costs almost nothing and still blocks most fakes.

Each idea added to v0.4 alone, 32 paired seeds (day 30 for v0.4 is 69.0%). A switch that changes nothing but the random numbers moved day 30 by −0.4, so treat differences under about 1.5 points as noise.

| Ladder pacts | +2.5 | +2.8 |
| **Midnight, batched at 00:00** | +2.3 | +1.5 |
| Wall of Feats, best circles | +1.9 | +1.9 |

**Building it up** (best remaining idea each step, 16 seeds): v0.4 69% → +Pacts 78% → +Mole 82% → +Wall 85% → +Midnight 87% → +Calls 88% → +Ladder 89% → +hours 89% → +circle Missions 90%. In 20 runs with every idea's strength drawn at random between half and one and a half times its default, Pacts made the top three 100% of the time and the Mole 95%.

**Flow: the same features, three ways to show them** (32 seeds). The cost is per concept beyond the nine v0.3 asked people to learn.

| Cost per concept | v0.4 as built | v0.5 all at once (20 concepts) | Merged (14) | Merged, unlocked over 2 weeks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 69.0% | 89.3% | 89.3% | 89.3% |
| low | 67.8% | 85.4% | 88.0% | 88.4% |
| medium | 64.9% | 73.3% | 86.3% | 86.8% |
| high | 63.6% | **60.2%** | 81.6% | **84.7%** |

Shown all at once, at a high cost per concept, the new features end up below v0.4. Merged and unlocked gradually, they keep almost everything. Day-7 retention shows it more sharply: 69% unlocked vs 50% all at once.

## 6. What the model can't see
- **The levels are too high.** About 90% on day 30 has no precedent; the model saturates. Trust the ranking and the comparisons.
- **It can't see a confusing screen.** "Concept load" is a stand-in. The real test is five people using the phone for the first time, with you silent.
- **It doesn't read Midnight messages.** The 3% (about yourself) and 15% (about others) rates of hurtful messages are guesses. The gap between them is the finding.
- **The Mole lives or dies on its task list.** A bad list makes it a chore.

## 7. Real-world test
Add to the v0.4 test in [`IMPACT.md`](IMPACT.md#real-world-test-for-the-top-three), run with two circles for two weeks:
- **Onboarding:** watch five new people go through the first five minutes without help. Pass if all five start a pact and nobody asks "what is this for?"
- **Pacts:** everyone starts one. Pass if at least 70% are still checking in on day 14, and nobody says a pact felt like a debt.
- **The Mole:** two rounds. Pass if the circle asks for a third.
- **Midnight:** one week. Pass if nobody can name an author, and no message gets removed for being about someone else.
- **A Call:** one circle posts one; the other answers.
