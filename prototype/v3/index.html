Hooks got us to 23%. To get to 90%, the app has to become the thing your friends can't leave.

Only one kind of app keeps around 90% of its users: the one your people talk and pay through. So v0.3 does two things at once. The ledger becomes your circle's everyday money, which is the boring part that keeps people. And the city becomes the board, which is the part that earns the name: Zones where you can go big on camera with staff on site, Hunts that circles build for each other, Drops of real stuff, and a League where your circle has a fixture every week.

90% of people opening on exactly day 30 isn't something any app has. 90% still around in month one is, and only apps that are infrastructure get there.

Typical social apps keep 4–10% of users on day 30. The one app reported near 90% month-one retention is WhatsApp, at about 86% (Sensor Tower, via a secondhand report). Nobody gets there with streaks or prizes. WhatsApp got there because leaving means losing the people you talk to.

So v0.3 adds two layers. Infrastructure: the ledger becomes the money your circle runs on, and the League gives your circle a fixture every week. Spectacle: Zones, Hunts and Drops get people out into the city together and make the app worth its name. The chart shows what each layer adds, one at a time, in the calibrated simulator.

Read the two numbers differently. Day 30 is calibrated against published benchmarks. Month one (opened on any day from 23 to 30) is looser: a plain app with no hooks already scores about 39% on it. v0.3 clears 90% on month one; on day 30 it reaches the high 50s, well above every published social-app range, so treat it as the optimistic end.

What carries the number

  • The ledger as money is the biggest single step. When your groceries, dinners and chores are settled in favors, you open the app for the same reason you open a banking app.
  • The League works like a five-a-side team. Teammates notice when you're missing and pull you back, which is something no notification can do.
  • Zones add a weekly night out with your circle, and give the feed fresh local content so it never goes stale.

What earns the name but not the number

  • Hunts and Drops add only 1–3 points each in the model. They're the stories people tell, the reason a stranger installs, and the screenshot on the poster.
  • That split is normal. Pokémon GO's daily users fell by about a third within weeks of its peak, and HQ Trivia's prize-driven crowd evaporated. Spectacle recruits; obligation retains.

The two things that would sink it

  • Removing the insanity ceiling. In a network this dense, escalation runs away: the simulator injures about 690 people in 120 days without it, against about 5 with it. Zones allow a higher ceiling (8 instead of 6.5) only because staff, padding and fixed cameras are there.
  • Paying drops in favors. That's free issuance, the same mistake as the trueque clubs and Bunz. Confidence in favors falls from 85% to about 50%.

What surprised the model

  • Chapters only work on top of Zones. On v0.2 they add nothing to retention, though they still grow the network. Without chapter nights, a private invite-only club is just a smaller feed.
  • Missions are the growth idea. Their retention gain is middling, but they bring in the most new people of anything tested, because a synchronized freeze in a station is the poster.
  • Duels are the only trust fix. They barely move retention, but favor confidence rises about 8 points: disputes get settled instead of rotting.
  • Homework helps without Zones. It works just as well on v0.2, so it's the cheapest thing to ship first.

The no-gos, confirmed

  • Favor cash-out is the only idea that loses users: favor confidence falls about 35 points. It's the IOU-selling failure of v0.1 again, with money attached.
  • Real fights add about a point and a half of retention and roughly 40 more people hurt in 120 days.
  • Pay-to-enter prizes add nothing measurable and turn every Drop into an illegal lottery.
  • A money partner that fails wipes out the whole Pots gain and knocks swaps down for weeks.

What the model can't see

  • It counts opens. It can't tell an evening people loved from one they felt they had to show up to, so the diary test still decides.
  • Every idea gets the value its rules imply. Nothing in the model is a bad execution of a good idea, and in real life most are.
  • Levels above 70% on day 30 have no real-world precedent. Trust the ranking, not the level.

What happened in this run

    Same user as the v0.1 and v0.2 diaries, one week on v0.3. Opened 7 of 7 days. The difference isn't that each day is more exciting. Every day has something the circle needs from you.

    Mon4 min
    Ledger

    Pia logged the week's groceries: six ways, I owe half a favor. Settled it by offering to cook Tuesday. That's how money works now.

    Tue6 min
    Ledger · League

    Cooked for four, so each eater owes me a quarter. Thursday's fixture is set: 4B & co vs Third Floor at Zone Night. We need four in the lineup. I tapped in.

    Wed11 min
    Hunt

    Third Floor's hunt, "The Lost Toaster": five clues across the neighborhood. Clue 2 needed a 10-second dare filmed at the bakery mural. Jules and I did three clues after work.

    Thu9 min + 2 h out
    Zone · Fixture

    Zone Night at The Loading Dock, a skatepark that partners with the app: staff, padded ramp, fixed cameras, ceiling 8. Our entry was a slow-motion action-movie fight with foam swords, scored 7.4. We won the fixture 3–2. Best night of the month.

    Fri5 min
    Drop

    A bike shop dropped a refurbished bike at the river steps. Claiming it needed three of us there together within two hours. Maya, Rin and I made it. The bike goes into Sunday's Market pool.

    Sat2 min
    Quiet

    Barely opened. Rin pinged the circle about Sunday. No streak, no decay, no guilt.

    Sun8 min + 1 h out
    Market

    Market at Zone 2. Four loops for me, including the drop bike going to someone in another circle for two hours of their help moving house.

    Who v0.3 is not for, and what keeps them anyway

    The trader (41, parent)

    Won't go to Zone Night, won't hunt. Still opens most days, because the ledger settles the school-run rota and the favor board finds a babysitter. In v0.1 they quit on day 9.

    The performer (19, student)

    Lives at the Zones. The ceiling of 8 with staff present gives them somewhere to go big. In v0.1 the same person was escalating on the street with nobody watching.
