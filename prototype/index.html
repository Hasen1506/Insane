What happened in this run

    Every loop below is a real, documented lever from behavioral psychology. Each one is drawn as it would ship: what the user sees, and what it turns into when nobody puts a ceiling on it. The simulator lets you switch most of them on and off.

    LoopIn the appWhen it runs unchecked

    Barter failed historically for three reasons: you need someone who has what you want and wants what you have, you can't save a goat for later, and nobody agrees what a goat is worth. INSANE answers each one with software, and the answers rebuild money by accident.

    Problem 1: the double coincidence of wants

    You have a bike and want guitar lessons. Maya teaches guitar but wants sourdough. Dev bakes and wants a bike. No pair can trade. The app finds the cycle (you → Dev → Maya → you) the way kidney-exchange programs match donors. Three-way and four-way loops raise the match rate from roughly 1 in 10 to roughly 1 in 2 in the simulator.

    Problem 2: no store of value

    When a loop is uneven, the difference becomes a favor: a signed IOU. Favors can be held, gifted, and sold. That is a store of value, and once IOUs circulate between strangers they work exactly like money. Anthropologists have argued credit came before coins. The app replays that history in about six weeks.

    Problem 3: no unit of account

    Nobody agrees what a haircut is worth against a PS4. The app shows a favor estimate for every item, computed from recent swaps of similar things. The estimate quietly becomes the price everyone anchors to.

    Dares as labor

    A dare is a payment in attention. You perform; the crowd watches; the person who issued the dare gets entertainment and status, and you get the stake. The insanity ranking sets the exchange rate between embarrassment and goods.

    The future nobody plans for doesn't arrive as a collapse. It arrives as a feature update. Each beat below is one small, reasonable product decision. Read together, they describe a society where your friendships carry interest.

    Season 1 · “Swap, don't spend”

    Launches as a sustainable, anti-consumerist barter app for friends. Press loves it. The ledger is private.

    Season 2 · Favors go public

    To “build trust,” ledgers become visible to friends. Everyone can see who owes whom. Repayment rates jump 40%. So does anxiety.

    Season 3 · The IOU market

    Users ask to sell IOUs they'll never collect. Collectors appear, buying thousands of favors at 60% of face value. Your friend's debt to you is now owned by @kestrel_collects, who you have never met, and who would like a dare.

    Season 4 · Proxy dares

    Holders of your IOU can settle it by assigning you a dare. The feed rewards insanity. Collectors with large books run “dare farms,” filming debtors for views that pay in more favors.

    Season 5 · Trust becomes a credit score

    Landlords ask for your Trust. Employers check your Nerve. Your score drops while you sleep, because decay was the best retention lever the team ever shipped.

    The thing staring us in the face

    Money never disappears. When it loses value, it comes back as obligation between people, and software makes those obligations tradeable, rankable, and permanent. The app isn't the dystopia. The ledger is.

    Design rules this prototype keeps, so it can ship

    • Insanity is scored on absurdity, creativity and commitment. Physical danger is a separate flag that removes reward, never adds it.
    • No dares involving strangers who didn't opt in, law-breaking, substances, heights, traffic, fire, or minors. Under-18 accounts get swaps only.
    • Any dare can be declined for free. Buying out costs a favor, never Trust.
    • Debt jubilee every 30 days for IOUs under a set size. Nobody's friendship carries interest forever.
    • IOUs are transferable only to people already in your friend graph. No anonymous collectors.
    • Trust decay pauses at night and on any day you mark as off. Lanes cap at 5.
